# Scope expansion — BNPL, credit cards, authorised overdrafts

> **Directive:** CCD2 arts 2–4 — [`general-provisions.md#article-2`](../../extracts/directive/general-provisions.md#article-2).
> **Dutch implementation:** Wft 1:1, 1:20, 2:81 — [`36924_artikel-I-wft.md#wft-1-20`](../../extracts/transposition-nl/36924_artikel-I-wft.md#wft-1-20).

## What CCD2 requires
- Abolishes the CCD1 **€200 lower limit**; raises the ceiling to **€100,000** (art 2(2)(c)); some
  provisions still apply above €100,000 for unsecured residential-renovation credit (art 2(3)).
- Brings **BNPL, credit cards and authorised overdrafts** into scope.

## How the Netherlands implements it
- **"Verlenen van uitstel van betaling"** is added to the definition of credit (art 1:1 Wft), and the
  exceptions in art 1:20 Wft are removed — notably the exception for credit repayable within three months
  with only insignificant costs, used by BNPL.
- BNPL providers, deferred-debit-card issuers and authorised-overdraft ("rood staan") providers thus fall
  under the Wft and need an **AFM licence** (art 2:60 Wft) or, for intermediaries, art 2:80 Wft.
- A **narrow BNPL exception** remains in a new art 1:20 Wft where (i) the supplier itself grants the
  deferral (no third party, unless a platform), (ii) no interest/costs except limited late-payment costs,
  and (iii) full payment within **50 days** (14 days for large enterprises).
- MKB intermediaries with credit-broking as an ancillary activity are exempt; large suppliers brokering
  short-term BNPL face a lighter **registration** regime (art 2:81 Wft).

## AFM scope interpretation (20 Nov 2026)
AFM's **interpretation "gangbare betaal- en facturatievormen"** (20 Nov 2026 — supervisor stappenplan,
annex to the 23 Nov 2026 toezeggingen letter) is now the authoritative reading of the exception boundary —
see [`../transposition/supervision-afm.md`](../transposition/supervision-afm.md#afm-scope-interpretation-of-uitstel-van-betaling-20-nov-2026):
- **Continuous services / continuous delivery of the same goods are _not_ credit** (art 3(3) CCD2), but an
  **economic-substance test** stops one-off costs being "hidden" in a service subscription.
- **Platforms always fall under the credit rules** (new BW 7:57(1)(ab)) and cannot use the exception.
- The **≤50-day** tier is for physical shops + small/medium online suppliers; **large online non-platform**
  suppliers get a stricter **≤14-day** tier *and* may not sell the claim (not even within the group).
- **Factoring** (selling the receivable) is not credit; a **third-party BNPL** deferral the consumer chooses
  is — AFM weighs the whole construction, so buying receivables is not automatically exempt.
