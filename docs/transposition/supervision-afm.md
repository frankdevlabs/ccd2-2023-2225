# Supervision — AFM, and the BNPL market context

> The conduct supervisor's role and the market the new rules address.

## AFM as supervisor

- **AFM** (Autoriteit Financiële Markten) is the conduct supervisor for consumer credit. CCDII page:
  https://www.afm.nl/nl-nl/sector/kredietaanbieders/herziene-richtlijn-consumentenkrediet-ccdii
- Ahead of the act's adoption, AFM has decided to **accept licence applications** from credit providers so
  assessments can be done in time before **20 Nov 2026** — applicants bear the cost/risk, and licences
  **cannot be granted before the rules are finalised**.
- AFM is a non-voting member of the European supervisory college for Klarna.
- New supervisory tooling in the bill: **product-intervention powers** (art 1:77q Wft).
- **Licensing / registration hooks (AFM sector page, Jul 2026).** AFM now names the specific Wft duties the
  newly in-scope providers and intermediaries must check:
  - **Art 2:60 Wft** — vergunningplicht for providers of deferred-payment services ("buy now, pay later"),
    deferred-debit cards, and hire/lease with an option to buy.
  - **Art 2:81 Wft** — registratieplicht for **large intermediaries** in short-term BNPL credit (jointly
    with the providers of that credit).
  - **Art 2:80 Wft** — vergunningplicht for intermediaries in newly-supervised credit forms that fall under
    no exception/exemption/registration duty.
  - AFM will let credit providers **register their deferred-payment intermediaries**; process, conditions and
    costs to follow later in 2026.

## AFM CCDII supervisory expectations (sector page)

The AFM's CCDII sector page sets out what it expects of credit providers under the recast rules:

- **Creditworthiness** stays an **open norm** — AFM expects further specification via sector
  methodology (e.g. the **VFN** code) and says it is discussing the expected standard with the market.
- **Age verification** must use a **reliable source** ("betrouwbare bron") such as **iDIN**,
  operationalising the ban on credit to minors.
- **Early signalling** ("vroegtijdige signalering") of payment problems and referral to debt
  assistance; AFM will update its **"Consumenten en Incassotrajecten"** guidance.
- Previously exempt agreements (incl. "uitstel van betaling" / BNPL) come under **Wft** conduct
  supervision, bringing new providers and intermediaries into scope.

### Three ongoing obligations ("drie doorlopende verplichtingen")

A dedicated AFM sub-page sets out three *doorlopende verplichtingen* under CCDII (page:
https://www.afm.nl/nl-nl/sector/kredietaanbieders/ccdii--doorlopende-verplichtingen):

1. **Responsible lending / passend krediet** — proportional creditworthiness. For **short-term credit**
   (repayment within **3 months**) the provider must verify age **and** consult **BKR**, assess the
   consumer's financial position from verified information, and refuse if not suitable ("de
   kredietverstrekker verstrekt geen krediet als dat niet passend is"). For **doorlopend krediet** the
   provider must run an **update policy** to monitor that the credit remains appropriate.
   The **Jul 2026** page sharpens the proportional norm for short-term credit:
   - if **BKR** shows an **existing arrears** ("bestaande achterstand"), **no credit is provided**;
   - the financial-position assessment **may not rest solely on the consumer's credit history**, and **all
     information gathered must be verified** (e.g. income/assets);
   - AFM wants the sector to make **joint acceptance-policy agreements** to avoid a "**race to the bottom**",
     and treats lending that deviates from those agreements as a possible indication of irresponsible
     lending;
   - the current **Gedragscode BNPL** consumer-protection measures are expected to be retained — consulting
     the central **curatele-/bewindregister** and **blocking service on arrears**.
2. **Age verification** — "aan de hand van een **betrouwbare bron, zoals iDIN**".
3. **Preventive management / early signalling** — detect consumers in difficulty and refer them to debt
   assistance; AFM will update its **"Consumenten en Incassotrajecten"** guidance.

## AFM scope interpretation of uitstel van betaling (20 Nov 2026)

On **20 Nov 2026** AFM published its **interpretation of when "uitstel van betaling" falls under the new
credit rules** (annex to the minister's 23 Nov 2026 toezeggingen letter, 2026D60190/2026D60191 —
[pipeline](nl-pipeline.md)), fulfilling the toezegging at the 28 Sep 2026 wetgevingsoverleg and moties
Van Eijk/Schonis/Ceder and Bushoff. It takes the form of a **stappenplan**:

1. **Doorlopende dienstverlening / doorlopende levering of the same goods is _not_ credit** (art 3(3) CCD2)
   — gas/water/electricity, insurance with monthly premiums, internet/TV/telephone, OV- and
   parking-subscriptions and pay-after-use. **Economic-substance test:** form *and* purpose are weighed, so
   one-off costs may **not be "hidden" in a service subscription** (e.g. solar-panel installation or a heat
   pump billed as a multi-year monthly "service" fee is credit).
2. **Platforms always in scope** — an online marketplace offering both own and third-party products (new
   **BW 7:57(1)(ab)**) that offers deferred payment always provides credit; it cannot use the exceptions.
3. **Business-model tiers for the ≤50-day / ≤14-day exception** (new Wft 1:20 / BW 7:58(2)):
   - **physical shops + small/medium online** suppliers: deferral exempt if **≤50 days** from delivery, **no
     interest/costs** (only reasonable statutory collection costs), and **no third party** involved;
   - **large online, non-platform** suppliers: stricter — **≤14 days**, no interest/costs, no third party,
     **and the claim may not be sold** to a third party (not even within the same group).
4. **Factoring vs third-party BNPL.** Selling the receivable (factoring) does **not** make the deferral
   credit; but a BNPL third party at whom the **consumer** chooses the deferral does. AFM assesses the whole
   construction — with whom the consumer contracts, who is entitled to payment, and how payment/communication
   are arranged — so merely buying receivables is not automatically exempt factoring.

AFM will update the interpretation if case-law develops (the CJEU ultimately decides); responsibility for
self-assessment stays with the firm (`ondernemersloket@afm.nl`).

**Enforcement coulance.** With entry into force now expected **begin 2027** (the 20 Nov 2026 target was
missed), the minister confirmed AFM will apply a **coulanceperiode** in prioritising supervision directly
after inwerkingtreding — **no full postponement** of supervision, but weighted by how **foreseeable** each
norm was (rules flowing straight from the directive, age verification and the late-payment-cost cap were
long foreseeable; genuinely new/unclear norms get more leeway, and firms that could not obtain a licence in
time are helped to a "redelijke oplossing" if they act promptly). AFM will address this in its **AFM Agenda
2027** (Jan 2027). AFM already takes licence applications in advance.

## BNPL market (AFM Marktupdate 2025, June 2025)

- In 2024 the four providers (**Billink, in3, Klarna, Riverty**) processed "**53 miljoen
  BNPL-transacties… goed voor €5,1 miljard**" (up ~17%).
- AFM supports lowering the **€250 BKR threshold** because "9 op de 10 transacties met betalingsproblemen
  een waarde van onder de €250 hebben"; the current BKR check "geldt voor kredieten hoger dan €250 met een
  looptijd van langer dan één maand."
- "**1 op de 6** [BNPL-gebruikers onder de 35] staat op minstens 90 dagen per jaar rood."
- The three e-commerce platforms (Amazon, Bol, Zalando) had 4.4 million Dutch deferred-payment users in
  2024 vs ~5.5 million for the four BNPL providers.
- AFM supports rapid **statutory age verification**.
- Report (PDF): https://www.afm.nl/~/profmedia/files/rapporten/2025/rapport-marktupdate--bnpl-2025-ned.pdf

## BNPL self-regulation (interim)

Pending the act's application the cabinet relies on sector self-regulation via the **Gedragscode BNPL**.
AFM names four NL signatory providers: **Billink, in3, Klarna and Riverty** (noting "Tinka, which is no
longer active"). Government statement (31 Oct 2025):
https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2025/10/31/nieuwe-regels-voor-buy-now-pay-later
